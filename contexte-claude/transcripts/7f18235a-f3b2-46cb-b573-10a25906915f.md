## Claude  ·  2026-08-20T08:21:50.071Z

Your intuition would be right if the SB error were a *discretization* error. The data says it isn't — and the sweep contains a result that pins down why. Look at what happens when you vary the drift at fixed h=0.025:

| γ | null | oblique | ffd |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-3 | 3.366e-3 | 3.369e-3 | 3.414e-3 |
| 5e-4 | 3.299e-3 | 3.230e-3 | 3.276e-3 |
| 2e-4 | 3.319e-3 | 3.146e-3 | 3.200e-3 |
| 1e-4 | 4.182e-3 | 3.124e-3 | 3.183e-3 |
| 1e-5 | 1.099e-2 | 3.317e-3 | 3.212e-3 |

**A crude analytic θ-β-M formula and a fitted 200-DOF FFD registration give the same SB answer to within 2%.** And the null drift — no β at all — matches them down to γ=2e-4, where it starts diverging for the documented kernel-collapse reason. So β is not contributing accuracy. It is only keeping the kernel alive.

That is the answer to "why does it lose to FFD," and it's structural, not a bug:

**1. The γ-anneal is deliberately erasing β.** β enters the SB objective as a KL against the reference path measure, weighted by γ. As γ→0, SB → pure entropic OT and forgets the drift entirely. You anneal down twelve stages precisely to reach the OT limit — which is the limit in which the FFD information that was giving the good answer has been thrown away. The SB then returns a map determined by the two marginals alone, and that map is worse than the registration it was seeded with. Consistent with this, SB/FFD bottoms out at 1.41 near γ=1e-4 and *rises* again below (1.46 at γ=1e-6). There's a floor and it sits above FFD.

**2. Objective mismatch.** SB is optimal for transporting the Hessian-eig proxy density; you score L2 on the Mach field. That proxy is a shock-strength indicator, i.e. mass on a near-curve. OT between curve-supported measures is tangentially degenerate — sliding mass *along* the shock costs almost nothing, so the tangential component of the map is set by density noise rather than physics. FFD's B-spline deformation is regularized to a smooth global map, which encodes the right correspondence by construction.

**Now, why refinement hurts.** The marginal is ρ ∝ |λ_max(H_M)|^{1/3} ([SB/utils.py:200-205](SB/utils.py#L200-L205)), and at a captured shock λ_max ~ ΔM/h². Refining doesn't just sharpen the target — it drives the marginals toward measures supported on curves. Entropic OT between near-singular marginals at fixed γ is exactly where the log-domain kernel blows up, and it does, spectacularly:

| h | γ=1e-4: den_T_max | gate reverts T / S | IPFP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.025 | 5.9e178 | 28.2% / 21.7% | converged |
| 0.0175 | 2.1e255 | 46.8% / 25.6% | converged |
| 0.0125 | 2.1e273 | 41.5% / 24.8% | **not converged** (res 1.2e-2 vs tol 1e-7, hit cap) |

Two consequences. First, to hold the blur-to-shock-thickness ratio √(2γ)/h fixed you need **γ ∝ h²** — γ=1e-4 at h0.025 corresponds to 2.5e-5 at h0.0125. You held γ fixed, so the relative smearing doubled across the ladder. Second, and this is a concrete code issue: [`_trust_gate`](SB/utils.py#L936-L948) reverts collapsed cells to `bary`, the **identity** — even in the drifted branch, where Φ_β is sitting right there as `phi_fwd`. So on ~40% of cells at h0.0125 the "SB map" is literally zero transport, i.e. the Linear baseline. That is the mechanistic reason BaryCDI's h-scaling tracks Linear's (+36% from h0.025→h0.0125) instead of FFD's (+9%).

Also flagging: the h0.0125/γ=1e-4 corner never converged, so that number is a lower bound on the damage, not a measurement. But h0.0125/γ=1e-3 *did* converge and is still worse than h0.025/γ=1e-3 (4.75e-3 vs 3.41e-3), so non-convergence isn't the whole story.

Four things worth trying, roughly in order of cost:

1. **Gate fallback to Φ_β instead of identity** — one line. Makes SB degrade to FFD where the kernel collapses instead of to Linear. Sharpest available test of the h-scaling mechanism, and should close a good part of the gap at fine h.
2. **γ ∝ h² ladder** instead of the fixed prefix. Directly tests the blur-vs-thickness hypothesis; costs one rerun.
3. **Stop annealing at the optimum.** The value of β lives at *finite* γ; you're driving past the sweet spot on purpose. γ≈2e-4 at h0.025 is where oblique/ffd bottom out.
4. **Run SBsquared at the fine meshes** — it's the one mechanism in the codebase that breaks point 1, since it re-injects the previous stage's own drift each stage rather than letting γ wash β out. `run_mesh_study.sbatch` defaults to `DRIFTS=ffd`, so it has never been run here: `DRIFTS="ffd SBsquared" sbatch run_mesh_study.sbatch`.
